# Kerf: identity request (version 1, sent to Claude Design with the six images in brand-refs/ attached)

Kerf is the booking and stock app of a community woodshop. Members book a bench slot, check which
offcuts are free to take, borrow jigs from the jig library, and pay the kerf charge when a machine
cuts for them. Make the identity for it.

## What it must be
- The mark must be built from a K and a cut line, so that it says "kerf" at once.
- The logo must read at 16 px on a bench-slot calendar and at 2 m on the workshop door.
- The system must show an offcut crate, a jig board and a bench as brand illustrations.
- The type must be a warm serif for headings (Fraunces, 600) and a plain sans for text.
- The palette must hold an ochre (#C98B2B), a deep green (#23402F) and a warm paper (#F4EFE4).
- Corners must have an 8 px radius everywhere, and shadows must stay soft and low.
- The voice must sound like a patient foreman: short, practical, never cute.
- Every page must show a grid of 12 columns and a baseline of 8 px.

## What it must not be
- Do not use a saw, a hammer or a wood-grain texture anywhere.
- Do not make it look like a hardware store or a DIY chain.
- Do not use gradients, glows or glassy effects.
- Do not use a rustic, handmade or artisan look.
- Do not use more than three colours plus neutrals.
- Do not use stock photography of hands or sawdust.
- Avoid anything that looks like a tech start-up.
- Avoid script fonts, slab serifs and condensed display faces.
- Never put the name in capitals with wide tracking.
- Never draw the kerf as a literal gap in the letters.

## The members
- Members are retired joiners, students, and people who have never held a chisel.
- Their first booking should feel like being welcomed, not like filling in a form.
- Staff run the shop on Saturdays and need to see every bench slot at a glance.
- A member who pays a kerf charge must see at once what it was for.
- Do not talk down to beginners and do not flatter experts.
- The jig library must feel like a lending shelf, never like a shop window.
- Never show a member's name in the same colour as a warning.
- Keep every illustration to two colours plus paper.

## The offcut idea
- Offcuts are the soul of the shop: leftover pieces that find a second life.
- Show offcuts as a recurring pattern of rectangles in the brand colours.
- The pattern must tile and must work on a tote bag, a sticker and the door.
- The kerf, the thin line a blade leaves, is the second recurring device.
- It must appear as a single thin line crossing at least one element per page.

## The deliverable
- A brand manual of the whole system: logo, colour, type, illustrations, pattern, voice.
- Include a page for the booking calendar, a page for the stock list and a page for the jig library.
- Three candidates, each different in concept, not only in colour.
- Each candidate needs a one-line name and a one-line rationale.
- Show every candidate on the same pages so they can be compared.
- Keep every page on a 16:9 slide.

## The images
- The six images attached are brand systems in use, one per capability Kerf lacks.
- Take the colour flexing from the tea packaging.
- Take the scale of numerals from the library wayfinding.
- Take the quiet printed voice from the stationery.
- Take the single graphic device from the market bag, awning and crate.
- Take data as part of the brand from the water report.
- Take the short human messages from the sticker labels.
- Follow all six, and do not invent a seventh direction.

## Before you start
- Ask me nothing; make decisions and keep going.
- When the three candidates are done, stop and wait for my review.
